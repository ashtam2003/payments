What Is a Credit Card?

A credit card lets you borrow money from the issuing bank up to a pre-approved limit. You repay monthly — in full or installments. Interest applies if you carry a balance.

How It Works

  • Bank lends money for purchases
  • Monthly statement with due date
  • Pay in full — zero interest
  • Pay partial — interest 18–36%/yr

Benefits

  • Builds credit history & score
  • Strong fraud protection
  • Rewards / cashback programs
  • Accepted globally via networks

Luhn Algorithm (Card Number Format Validation)

👉 A checksum algorithm used to verify if a card number is valid in format (not fake/typo).

It does NOT check if card has money. It only checks if the number is mathematically valid.

Authentication, Authorization & Settlement

🔐 Authentication — "Who Are You?"

Verifying the cardholder's identity.

  • CVV verification
  • 3D Secure / OTP
  • Verified by Visa / SecureCode
  • PIN entry (chip cards)

✅ Authorization — "Can They Pay?"

Checking the bank: sufficient credit? No fraud flags?

  • Bank checks balance/credit
  • Checks fraud flags
  • Approves or declines
  • Places hold on amount
Key Order

Authentication first (who are you?), then Authorization (do you have the money?). Can pass auth but still be declined for insufficient funds.

Authorization vs Settlement

🔒 Authorization — Temporary Hold

Bank says "Approved." Money reserved but NOT transferred.

  • Happens in ~2 seconds
  • Customer sees "Pending"
  • Hold expires ~7 days if not captured

✅ Settlement — Actual Transfer

Actual movement of funds to merchant's bank.

  • 1–3 business days after capture
  • Charge becomes permanent
  • Fees deducted at this stage

Automated Clearing House (ACH)
1
What Is ACH?

ACH is a US electronic bank-to-bank network operated by NACHA. Moves money directly between bank accounts — no Visa/MC involved.

📋 ACH Requires

  • Routing Number — 9-digit ABA bank identifier
  • Account Number — checking or savings
  • Account Type — Checking or Savings
  • Account Holder Name
  • Transaction Type — Debit (pull) or Credit (push)

✅ Why Use ACH?

  • Much lower fees (~$0.25–$1.50 flat)
  • No interchange or card network fees
  • Ideal for recurring, large payments
  • Direct deposit & payroll
  • Available in Authorize.net as eCheck

Basics
What Is a Check?
A written order directing a bank to pay a specific amount to a payee.

A check instructs your bank to pay from your checking account to the named payee once presented and cleared.

📋 A Check Must Contain

  • Date of issue
  • Payee name (recipient)
  • Amount in numbers AND words
  • Drawer's authorized signature
  • Bank routing + account number (MICR)

✅ When Checks Are Used

  • Rent payments
  • B2B payments
  • Real estate transactions
  • Payroll (paycheck)
  • Government payments & refunds
Still Relevant?

Checks account for billions in US transactions annually — especially B2B and real estate. Banks process them via ACH image conversion.

Check Printing & Pre-Printed Check Formats

In real-world business systems, companies do not print complete checks from scratch. Instead, they use pre-printed checks that already contain company and bank details.

Key benefits

  • Faster printing with consistent layout
  • Legal compliance with bank MICR information
  • Flexible ERP alignment for top/middle/bottom formats

What are Pre-Printed Checks?

  • Checks ordered from vendors with company logo
  • Contain bank account number and routing number
  • Designed in specific layouts (top, middle, bottom check positions)

How Companies Order Pre-Printed Checks

  • Ordered from online check printing vendors
  • Customized with:
    • Company name and logo
    • Bank account number
    • Routing number
  • Delivered as ready-to-use check books

Role of Stone Profit System (ERP)

  • Does not print full check design
  • Prints only dynamic data:
    • Payee name
    • Amount (numbers and words)
    • Date
    • Memo

Check Format Customization

  • ERP supports multiple check layouts
  • Formats include:
    • Top check format
    • Middle check format
    • Bottom check format
  • System aligns printed data with pre-printed fields

This flexibility ensures that the printed information correctly matches the layout of the pre-printed check paper used by the company.

In real systems, checks are pre-printed with company and bank details. The ERP system like Stone Profit only prints variable data like payee and amount. Since different check layouts exist, the system provides configurable formats to align printing correctly.

B3
Types
Types of Checks
Different checks serve different purposes and offer different levels of guarantee.

Personal Check

From individual's checking account. Bank honors if funds available. Most common type.

Cashier's Check

Issued by the bank itself — drawn on bank's own funds. Guaranteed payment. Used for real estate, large purchases.

Certified Check

Personal check bank certifies — verifies signature and sets aside funds. More secure than personal check.

Business Check

Drawn from business checking account. Preprinted with company name, address, logo.

Money Order

Purchased upfront for specific amount. No bank account needed. Max $1,000 (USPS). Used by unbanked.

Traveler's Check

Pre-signed, used as safe cash for travel. Largely replaced by prepaid debit cards. AmEx was largest issuer.

Check 21 Act (2004)

Allows banks to transmit a digital image of a check instead of the physical paper. This is why mobile deposit works — your phone captures the image and the bank transmits it electronically.

⚠️ Stale Check

Checks older than 6 months may be rejected as "stale-dated." Banks can refuse to honor them.

Stop Payment

Drawer can instruct their bank to refuse a check before it clears. Fee applies (~$25–$35). Valid 6 months.

A real-world payment scenario — connecting all concepts together

Story Line for payments

Below are both: standard payment patterns and the three real-world storyline scenarios.

General payment types

Payments Story line Flow
1. AUTH ONLY You go to a hotel and swipe your card at check-in. The hotel does not charge you yet. It just checks if you have money and blocks it temporarily. If you cancel or leave, the money is released. Auth → Hold placed → No Capture → No Settlement → Hold released
2. AUTH + CAPTURE (DELAYED) You order something online. The website checks your card and blocks the money. The item is shipped later. Once shipped, the company actually charges you. Auth → Hold → Capture (later) → Settlement
3. SALE (INSTANT PAYMENT) You buy something at a shop. You swipe your card and the money is deducted instantly. No waiting. Auth + Capture (same time) → Settlement
4. PRE-AUTHORIZATION You check into a hotel. They block ₹5000 even though your room is ₹3000. After checkout, they adjust and charge the final amount. Auth (extra hold) → Adjust amount → Capture → Settlement
5. PARTIAL CAPTURE You order 3 items online. Only 2 are available. The company only charges for 2 items. Auth (₹1000) → Capture (₹700) → Settlement
6. MULTIPLE CAPTURE You order multiple items. They are delivered separately. Each time an item is shipped, you are charged partially. Auth → Capture 1 → Capture 2 → Settlement
7. VOID (CANCEL BEFORE CHARGE) You swipe your card but immediately cancel the transaction. The bank removes the hold and no money is taken. Auth → Void → No Capture → No Settlement
8. REFUND (AFTER PAYMENT) You buy a product and later return it. The company sends your money back to your account. Sale → Settlement → Refund (new transaction)
9. CHECK Every payment is just a journey of money: first the bank checks, then the merchant charges, then the money is transferred. Auth -> "Check and hold" Capture → “Charge” Settlement -> "Transfer"

Three detailed scenarios

1. Petrol Bunker / Gas Station

You go to a petrol pump and swipe your card. The system doesn’t know how much fuel you’ll fill, so it first blocks a fixed amount (say ₹5000). After fueling (say ₹2000), the actual amount is charged and the rest is released..

Date Time Amount Transaction Type What Happens

Day 1 10:05 ₹2000 Capture Actual fuel amount charged
Day 1 EOD ₹2000 Settlement Money sent to petrol pump
Day 2 — ₹3000 Release Remaining hold released

2. Hotel (Room + Incidentals)

You check into a hotel. They block money for room charges and extra expenses (incidentals like food, minibar). At checkout, they charge the final amount based on what you used.

Date Time Amount Transaction Type What Happens
Day 1 14:00 ₹10000 Authorization Room + incidentals hold at check-in
Day 2 — — No action Stay continues
Day 3 11:00 ₹8000 Capture Final bill charged on checkout
Day 3 EOD ₹8000 Settlement Money sent to hotel
Day 4 — ₹2000 Release Unused hold released

3. Car Rental (Rental + Fuel, Damage)

At pickup, rental + fuel and damage holds are placed. On return, the rental/fuel amount is captured and settled immediately. The damage hold remains pending and is captured or released after inspection (up to 15 days).

Day 1 09:00 ₹10000 Authorization Damage security hold

Day 5 EOD ₹12000 Settlement Money sent to rental company
Day 20 — ₹10000 Release Damage hold released (no issue)
Day 20* — ₹3000 Capture Damage charge (if damage found)
Day 20* EOD ₹3000 Settlement Damage amount transferred

*For damage scenario, charge/capture happens up to 15 days after return to allow inspection.
